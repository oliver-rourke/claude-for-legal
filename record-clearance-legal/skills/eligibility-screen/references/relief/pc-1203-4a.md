# PC 1203.4a: dismissal for a misdemeanor without probation, or an infraction

**Screened from v0.2.** Record fields used: `offense_level`, `probation_granted`, `judgment_date`, `new_conviction_since`, `code_section`, `status.current_supervision`, `status.pending_case`.

## What it is

The dismissal statute for misdemeanors where probation was not granted and for infractions. When its conditions are met the court permits withdrawal of the plea or sets aside the verdict and "shall dismiss the accusatory pleading," and the person "shall be released from all penalties and disabilities resulting from the offense," with the exceptions listed (subd. (a)).

## Who may qualify

- **Mandatory route** (subd. (a)): "every defendant convicted of a misdemeanor and not granted probation, and every defendant convicted of an infraction," at any time "after the lapse of one year from the date of pronouncement of judgment," if they "have fully complied with and performed the sentence of the court, are not then serving a sentence for an offense and are not under charge of commission of a crime, and have, since the pronouncement of judgment, lived an honest and upright life and have conformed to and obeyed the laws of the land."
- **Discretionary route** (subd. (b)): after one year from judgment, when not every requirement of (a) is met, the court "in its discretion and in the interest of justice, may grant the relief" if the person has complied with the sentence, is not serving a sentence, and is not under charge.
- The application may be made "in person or by attorney, or by the probation officer authorized in writing" (subd. (c)(1)).
- Unpaid restitution is not a ground for denial and does not show non-compliance with the sentence or a failure to live an honest and upright life (subd. (e)).

## Who is excluded

Subdivision (d): "a misdemeanor violation of subdivision (c) of Section 288," "a misdemeanor falling within the provisions of Section 42002.1 of the Vehicle Code," and "an infraction falling within the provisions of Section 42001 of the Vehicle Code."

## What the relief does and does not do

- The prior conviction "may be pleaded and proved" in a later prosecution (subd. (c)(1)).
- Dismissal "does not permit a person to own, possess, or have in their custody or control a firearm" (subd. (c)(2)) and does not restore eligibility for public office where the conviction barred it (subd. (c)(3)).
- Unexpired criminal protective orders remain in effect (subd. (c)(4)).
- Chapter 3 of Division 9 (firearms) and Vehicle Code section 13555 are carved out of the release (subd. (a)).

## Procedure notes

- Infraction petitions are "by written declaration, except upon a showing of compelling need," and need "at least 15 days' notice" to the prosecuting attorney (subd. (f)).
- The person must be told of this section at sentencing (subd. (c)(1)).

## Plain-language explanation

If you were convicted of a misdemeanor and the court did not put you on probation, or if the conviction was an infraction, you can ask the court to dismiss it one year after the judgment, as long as you did everything the sentence required, you are not serving a sentence or facing a new charge, and you have stayed out of trouble. If you meet all of that, the court must grant it. If you miss one piece, the judge can still grant it as a matter of discretion. The same limits apply as with other dismissals: government job and license applications still see it, gun rights do not come back, and it can count against you in a later case.
