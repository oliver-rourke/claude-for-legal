# PC 1203.41: dismissal after a felony sentence served in county jail or state prison

**Screened in v0.1 and v0.2: yes.** Record fields used: `offense_level`, `sentence`, `probation_granted`, `sentence_completed`, `notes`, `status.current_supervision`, `status.pending_case`, `status.registration_290`.

## What it is

A discretionary dismissal for felony convictions that did not end in a grant of probation: sentences served in county jail under PC 1170(h), split sentences with mandatory supervision, and state prison sentences. The court "in its discretion and in the interest of justice, may" permit withdrawal of the plea or set aside the verdict and dismiss the accusation, releasing the person "from all penalties and disabilities resulting from the offense" except as provided in Vehicle Code section 13555 (subd. (a)(1)).

## Who may qualify

- A waiting period must have run since "completion of the sentence": one year for a sentence imposed under PC 1170(h)(5)(B) (a split sentence with mandatory supervision); two years for a sentence under PC 1170(h)(5)(A) or a state prison sentence (subd. (a)(2)). The cross-reference to (h)(5)(A) is read as a straight county jail term: since 2015 the text of (h)(5)(A) describes the court's duty to suspend a concluding portion of the term, and the one-year and two-year split follows the common practitioner reading `[model knowledge — verify]`.
- The person must not be "on parole or under supervision pursuant to subparagraph (B) of paragraph (5) of subdivision (h) of Section 1170," and must not be "serving a sentence for, on probation for, or charged with the commission of, an offense" (subd. (a)(3)).
- For a felony that resulted in a state prison sentence, relief is available only if the felony "did not result in a requirement to register as a sex offender pursuant to Chapter 5.5 (commencing with Section 290)" (subd. (a)(6)).
- The section applies to qualifying convictions "that occurred before, on, or after January 1, 2021" (subd. (c)).
- The application may be made "in person or by attorney, or by a probation officer authorized in writing" (subd. (a)(5)).

## Who is excluded

- Anyone still inside the waiting period, still on parole or mandatory supervision, still serving a sentence or on probation, or facing a pending charge (subds. (a)(2), (a)(3)).
- State prison felonies that carry a PC 290 registration requirement (subd. (a)(6)).

## What the relief does and does not do

- The prior conviction "may be pleaded and proved and shall have the same effect as if the accusation or information had not been dismissed" in any later prosecution (subd. (b)(1)).
- The person must still disclose the conviction "in response to a direct question contained in a questionnaire or application for public office, for licensure by a state or local agency or by a federally recognized tribe," for enrollment as an in-home supportive services provider, or for contracting with the California State Lottery Commission (subd. (b)(2)).
- Dismissal "does not permit a person to own, possess, or have in their custody or control a firearm" (subd. (b)(3)) and does not restore eligibility to hold public office where the conviction barred it (subd. (b)(4)).
- Unexpired criminal protective orders remain in effect (subd. (g)).
- Agencies that receive criminal history information for care licensing and similar purposes keep that authority (subd. (h)).

## Procedure notes

- "Relief shall not be granted under this section unless the prosecuting attorney has been given 15 days' notice of the petition for relief"; the probation officer notifies the prosecuting attorney when a petition is filed, and proof of service creates a presumption of notice (subd. (e)).
- If the prosecuting attorney, after notice, "fails to appear and object," the prosecutor "shall not move to set aside or otherwise appeal the grant of that petition" (subd. (f)).
- "An unpaid order of restitution or restitution fine shall not be grounds for denial of the petition for relief" (subd. (d)).
- Because the relief is discretionary, courts commonly expect a declaration describing the person's circumstances since the conviction `[model knowledge — verify]`.

## Facts the screen needs

Sentence type (split with mandatory supervision, straight jail, or prison), the month the sentence was completed (and, when release and supervision discharge differ, both months), current supervision status, any pending charge, and registration status. For a state prison term, PC 3000(a)(1) provides that the sentence includes a period of parole, so practitioners commonly count the two years from discharge from parole rather than release from custody `[model knowledge — verify]`; the screen shows both dates and leaves the choice to the attorney. When the applicant called a post-prison term "community supervision," staff must confirm whether it was mandatory supervision under PC 1170(h)(5)(B) or post-release community supervision after prison; the waiting period differs (one year versus two).

## Plain-language explanation

If you were sentenced to county jail or state prison for a felony, you may be able to ask the court to dismiss that conviction once some time has passed and you have finished your sentence. The wait is one year if your sentence was split between jail and supervision, and two years if it was straight jail time or prison. You cannot be on parole, on supervision, on probation, or facing a new charge when you apply. The judge decides whether to grant it, so you will usually need to explain how things have changed for you. A dismissal helps with many job and housing applications, but you still have to disclose the conviction for government jobs and licenses, it does not restore the right to own a gun, and it does not erase the conviction if you are ever charged again.
