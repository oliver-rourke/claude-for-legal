## PC 1203.4b: dismissal for fire camp, county hand crew and institutional firehouse participants

Applies to someone who "successfully participated in the California Conservation Camp program as an incarcerated individual hand crew member," "as a member of a county incarcerated individual hand crew," or "at an institutional firehouse," and "has been released from custody" (subd. (a)(1)). Automatically ineligible: murder, kidnapping, specified rape offenses, lewd acts on a child under 14, a felony punishable by death or life, a registrable sex offense, escape from a secure perimeter within ten years, arson (subd. (a)(1)(A) to (H)). The person "is not required to complete the term of their probation, parole, or supervised release," and the court "shall order early termination" when there have been no violations (subd. (b)(4)). Petition in the county of sentencing; CDCR or the county certifies participation (subd. (b)(1) to (3)); 15 days' notice to the prosecutor (subd. (e)). Not screened because certification facts are not on the intake; the intake's fire camp question already raises this flag. Referral line: "fire camp or hand crew participation reported; PC 1203.4b may allow relief even while on supervision, confirm certification." Amended by Stats. 2025, Ch. 746 (SB 245), effective 2026-01-01 (leginfo, fetched 2026-10-03). `[statute / regulator site]` `[settled — last confirmed 2026-10-04]`

## PC 1203.42: dismissal for felonies sentenced before realignment

For a felony sentenced before the 2011 Realignment Legislation that would now be eligible for a PC 1170(h) sentence; relief is discretionary, available two years after completion of the sentence, and only if the person is not under supervision, serving a sentence, on probation or charged; 15 days' notice to the prosecutor. Not screened because the fetch returned a summary and the realignment-eligibility question depends on the offense. Referral line: "pre-2011 felony; PC 1203.42 may apply, attorney to confirm realignment eligibility." Amended by AB 134, effective 2023-07-10 (leginfo summary). `[partial text, verify]`

## PC 1203.425: automatic conviction relief

See `screening-bands.md` section 5 for the conditions the flag uses. The Department of Justice reviews records monthly from October 1, 2024, "subject to an appropriation," grants relief "without requiring a petition" when its records show eligibility, and marks the record "relief granted" (subds. (a)(1)(A), (a)(2)(A), (a)(2)(B)). A prosecutor or probation department may petition to block relief up to 90 days before the eligibility date (subd. (b)(1)). Relief does not restore firearm rights, does not remove peace-officer and public-office disclosure duties, and leaves criminal justice agency access intact (subd. (a)(4)). Flag only, because the plugin never sees the DOJ record. Referral line: "check the RAP sheet for a 'relief granted' note before petitioning." `[statute text via public.law mirror, 2026-10-04 — verify at the leginfo link]`

## PC 17(b): reducing a wobbler felony to a misdemeanor

Where an offense is punishable either as a felony or as a misdemeanor, the court may declare it a misdemeanor at sentencing or later on application, through the routes listed in subdivision (b); the current text also addresses repeat applications and provides that unpaid restitution is not a ground for denial. A PC 290 registration requirement is not affected by the reduction. Not screened because whether an offense is a wobbler is a code-section question outside this plugin. Referral line: "felony that may be a wobbler; a PC 17(b) reduction is an attorney question." `[partial text, verify]`

## Proposition 47 (PC 1170.18): redesignating specified theft and drug felonies as misdemeanors

A person who completed a sentence for a felony that Proposition 47 made a misdemeanor may apply to have it designated a misdemeanor; a person still serving may petition for resentencing; the court may deny resentencing on a finding of "unreasonable risk of danger to public safety," defined by reference to new violent felonies; people with specified prior violent convictions or a registration requirement are excluded. Amended effective 2023-10-08 (leginfo summary). Not screened because eligibility turns on the specific offense and dollar amount, which is engine territory. Referral line: "theft or drug felony that may qualify under Proposition 47; route to full analysis." `[partial text, verify]`

## Proposition 64 (HSC 11361.8): marijuana convictions

A person currently serving a sentence for a marijuana offense "who would not have been guilty of an offense, or who would have been guilty of a lesser offense" under the Adult Use of Marijuana Act may petition for recall or dismissal (subd. (a)); a person who completed the sentence may apply to have the conviction dismissed and sealed or redesignated (subd. (e)); the court presumes the petitioner qualifies unless the opposing party proves otherwise "by clear and convincing evidence" (subds. (b), (f)); juvenile adjudications qualify on the same terms (subd. (m)). Not screened because it is offense-specific. Referral line: "marijuana conviction; Proposition 64 relief likely available, route to full analysis." `[statute text via public.law mirror, 2026-10-04 — verify at the leginfo link]`

## PC 851.91: sealing an arrest that did not result in a conviction

Available when "the statute of limitations has run on every offense upon which the arrest was based" and no accusatory pleading was filed, or a pleading was filed but every charge ended without a conviction (dismissed and cannot be refiled, acquitted, or reversed with no refiling) (subd. (a)(1)). Not available while the person may still be charged, for murder or another offense with no statute of limitations unless acquitted or found factually innocent, or where the person intentionally evaded prosecution (subd. (a)(2)). The petition is verified, filed where the pleading was filed or in the county of arrest, and served on the prosecutor and the arresting agency at least 15 days before the hearing (subd. (b)(1)). Sealing is "a matter of right" unless the arrest involved a pattern of domestic violence, child abuse or elder abuse, in which case the petitioner shows that sealing serves the interests of justice (subd. (c)). Judicial Council forms exist in five languages (subd. (b)(3)(A)). Not screened because the statute-of-limitations question is an attorney call. Referral line: "arrest with no conviction; PC 851.91 petition likely available once the attorney confirms the limitations period has run." The screen does not state what that period is. Amended by Stats. 2018, Ch. 653 (AB 2599), effective 2019-01-01 (leginfo). `[statute / regulator site]` `[settled — last confirmed 2026-10-04]`

## PC 851.93: automatic arrest record relief

From October 1, 2024, the Department of Justice reviews records monthly and grants arrest relief without a petition for arrests on or after January 1, 1973 where a misdemeanor case was dismissed, a misdemeanor arrest saw no conviction after one year, a felony arrest saw no conviction after three years (six years for offenses punishable by eight years or more), or the person completed a specified diversion program (subd. (a)(2)(A) to (D)). Relief does not affect prosecution within the limitations period, firearm authorization, or peace-officer disclosure (subd. (d)). Flag only. Referral line: "older arrest without conviction; automatic relief may already appear on the RAP sheet." `[statute text via public.law mirror, 2026-10-04 — verify at the leginfo link]`

## Certificate of rehabilitation (PC 4852.01, 4852.03)

A court-issued certificate that also serves as an application for a pardon. Generally available after a felony conviction (4852.01(a)); some registrable offenses dismissed under PC 1203.4 may qualify on specified conditions (4852.01(b)); offenses listed in 4852.01(c) are excluded. The period of rehabilitation is "five years' residence in this state" plus an additional four years for the most serious offenses, five years for registrable offenses, or two years for others (4852.03(a)(1) to (3)); no petition may be filed until the period has run (4852.03(b)). Not screened because the residence and conduct facts are not on the intake. Referral line: "felony with a long clean period; certificate of rehabilitation may be worth evaluating." `[statute text via public.law mirror, 2026-10-04 — verify at the leginfo link]`

## Vacatur for trafficking survivors (PC 236.14, PC 1203.49)

PC 236.14 lets a person arrested for or convicted of a nonviolent offense "while they were a victim of human trafficking" petition to vacate the conviction and seal the records on a showing "by clear and convincing evidence" that the offense was a direct result of the trafficking and that the person lacked the requisite intent; the prosecutor has 45 days to respond, a hearing follows unless unopposed, and the petition may be filed at any time after the person stops being a victim or seeks services (subds. (a), (b), (c), (f), (g), (l)). PC 1203.49 gives a person convicted of PC 647(b) prostitution who completed probation the PC 1203.4 relief on a clear-and-convincing showing that the conviction resulted from being trafficked. Related vacatur provisions exist for survivors of intimate partner violence and sexual violence `[model knowledge — verify]`. Not screened because these are fact-intensive and sensitive; the intake flag routes to staff follow-up. Referral line: "applicant reported trafficking or domestic violence remedies; route to an attorney for a confidential conversation." `[statute text via public.law mirror, 2026-10-04 — verify at the leginfo link]`

## Early termination of probation or mandatory supervision (PC 1203.3)

The court "has the authority at any time during the term of probation to revoke, modify, or change its order of suspension," and the same authority applies to mandatory supervision (subd. (a)); a modification requires a hearing with two days' written notice to the prosecutor (subd. (b)(1)), and the probation officer must be notified (subd. (b)(2)); restitution amounts are not reduced without extraordinary reasons. Relevant when an applicant is still on probation: early termination followed by a PC 1203.4 petition is a common path. Not screened because it is a motion, not a record relief. Referral line: "still on probation; early termination under PC 1203.3 may be worth raising with the attorney." `[statute text via public.law mirror, 2026-10-04 — verify at the leginfo link]`
