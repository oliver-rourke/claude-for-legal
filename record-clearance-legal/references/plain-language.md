# Plain-language explanations for staff

Sixth-grade reading level. Staff use these when they explain the process to applicants after the supervising attorney has reviewed the screen; nothing here is advice about a particular case. Every statement about the law points to a card in `skills/eligibility-screen/references/relief/`, where the statute text and its last-confirmed date live. Topic coverage was informed by public legal information guides, including Root & Rebound's Roadmap to Reentry, and the explanations were written fresh; the statute text controls.

## What a RAP sheet is

A RAP sheet is the state's record of your arrests and what happened after each one. In California the Department of Justice keeps it. It lists arrests, charges, what the court decided, and sentences. Courts, probation and some employers see different versions of it. Before anyone can tell you which convictions can be cleared, someone needs to read your RAP sheet, because memory and old paperwork often miss cases.

## How to get your own RAP sheet

You ask the Department of Justice for a "record review" copy. The steps, as described on The Access Project's public guidance page:

1. Check whether you qualify for a fee waiver on the Department of Justice website. You can qualify by income or by receiving public assistance such as Medi-Cal. You do not have to prove your income.
2. Fill out the form on the website. Your name in the personal information section must match the name in the signature section exactly, including capitalization.
3. Save, download and print the form. This form is not your RAP sheet; it is the request you take to a Live Scan operator.
4. Fill in the rest of the applicant information. Your mailing address must be correct, because the Department of Justice mails the RAP sheet to you. If you want someone else to get a copy, list them as a designee.
5. Take the form and a photo ID to a Live Scan location. Fees and hours vary; call first. After the scan, photograph the completed form as your receipt.
6. Wait for the RAP sheet in the mail.

If you did not qualify for the fee waiver you still request the record review the same way; you pay the Department of Justice fee and the Live Scan fee.

## What a dismissal does

In California, "expungement" usually means a dismissal under Penal Code section 1203.4 or a related section. The court lets you take back your guilty plea and dismisses the case. After that you can honestly say on most private job applications that you have not been convicted of that offense. See the PC 1203.4 and PC 1203.41 cards.

## What a dismissal does not do

- It does not erase the case from your RAP sheet; the entry stays, marked as dismissed.
- You still have to disclose the conviction when you apply for a government job, a state or local license, or a contract with the state lottery (PC 1203.4(a)(1)).
- It does not give back the right to own or possess a gun (PC 1203.4(a)(2)). If you have a felony conviction and want to understand your firearm rights, ask the attorney; possession after a conviction can be a new crime.
- It does not remove a conviction from your DMV record or shorten how long a DUI stays there (PC 1203.4 carves out Vehicle Code section 13555).
- If you are ever charged again, the dismissed conviction can still be used against you (PC 1203.4(a)(1)).
- Dismissal and immigration are different systems; a dismissed conviction may still count for immigration purposes `[model knowledge — verify]`. Anyone who is not a citizen should talk to an immigration attorney before filing anything.

## Mandatory and discretionary

Some requests the judge must grant: for example, a conviction where you were given probation and finished it with no violations (PC 1203.4(a)(1), first two routes). Others are up to the judge: for example, a dismissal after a prison sentence (PC 1203.41), or a probation case where probation was revoked. When the request is up to the judge, you will usually need to show how your life has changed. That is what a declaration is for.

## Waiting periods in plain words

- Finished probation: you can ask as soon as probation is over (PC 1203.4).
- A misdemeanor or infraction with no probation: one year after the judgment, if you completed the sentence (PC 1203.4a, screened from v0.2).
- A felony served in county jail with a split sentence and supervision: one year after you finished the whole sentence (PC 1203.41(a)(2)).
- A felony served as straight county jail time or in state prison: two years after the sentence is complete (PC 1203.41(a)(2)). Whether that date is release from custody or discharge from parole is a question for the attorney.
- You cannot be on probation, parole or supervision, serving a sentence, or facing a new charge when you ask (PC 1203.4(a)(1), PC 1203.41(a)(3)).

## If you are still on probation

You usually have to finish probation first. In some situations a court will end probation early, and the dismissal request can be heard at the same time (PC 1203.3, not screened in v0.2). People who served on a fire camp or county hand crew may qualify for relief without finishing supervision (PC 1203.4b, not screened in v0.2). Both are questions for the attorney.

## Automatic relief

Since October 2024 the Department of Justice reviews records every month and clears some convictions on its own, without a petition, when its records show the person qualifies (PC 1203.425). When that has happened, the RAP sheet says "relief granted" next to the entry. That is one more reason to get the RAP sheet before anyone files anything.

## Fines and restitution

Unpaid restitution or a restitution fine is not by itself a reason to deny a dismissal under PC 1203.4 or PC 1203.41 (PC 1203.4(c)(3), PC 1203.41(d)). Restitution is still owed.

## The declaration

When the judge has discretion, the request usually comes with a declaration. It has three parts, as The Access Project describes them in its program materials:

- **Your personal statement.** One to two pages, in your own words, to the judge: what was going on in your life when the convictions happened, and how things have changed since. Without it the judge sees only the record.
- **Supporting materials.** Documents that show you have moved on: school transcripts, certificates from treatment or training programs, proof of enrollment, awards for volunteer work.
- **Letters of support.** Like references: from supervisors, teachers, sponsors, clergy, coaches, family, people you have volunteered with. One to three letters is typical. They can be typed, handwritten or emailed. They help, but you can file without them.

Ask for letters early so people have time to write them.

## Who else can help

Record clearance help exists in most California counties, through public defender offices, legal aid organizations, law school clinics and reentry programs. The profile's `## Referral targets` section holds the clinic's current referral list and its out-of-county guidance. Convictions from other states and federal convictions follow other rules; this plugin does not screen them.
